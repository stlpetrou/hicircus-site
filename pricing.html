Credit Bank, Explained

How It Works

One Credit represents one hour of dedicated studio time, billed in half-hour increments. It's a way to prepay for a block of our time and spend it across as many or as few deliverables as your pipeline needs.

  1. 01

    Send your brief

    Tell us what you need. We assess it and send back an estimate of the credits required plus a delivery timeline.

  2. 02

    Approve & we begin

    Confirm the estimate and timeline in writing and we start, drawing down your balance as the work happens. Smaller ad-hoc requests are deducted directly once done.

  3. 03

    Top up or roll over

    Running low mid-project? Top up to cover the difference. Upgrade to a larger package before your current one expires and any unused credits carry over.

Good to Know

Questions

What exactly is a credit?

One Credit equals one hour of studio time under normal working hours, billed in 30-minute (0.5 credit) increments — whether that's a quick support request or time logged against a larger project.

Do credits expire?

Yes. 20 and 40-credit packages are valid for 2 months from activation; 80-credit and larger packages for 3 months. Unused credits lapse at the end of that window — unless you upgrade to a larger package before it expires, in which case any remaining balance carries over.

What if a project runs over my balance?

We'll flag it before you go over, never after. Work pauses until a top-up covers the difference, so there are no surprise invoices.

Can we mix the two models?

Yes. Many clients start with a single Per Project engagement, then move to a Credit Bank once the pipeline becomes ongoing.

How do we actually pay?

There's no online checkout. Per Project work is split 50% to begin, 50% on delivery; Credit Bank packages are prepaid in full before activation. Both are by bank transfer, arranged once we've agreed scope.

What's your cancellation policy?

Deposits and prepaid Credit Bank packages are non-refundable once a project is underway. Full details are in our Terms of Use.
